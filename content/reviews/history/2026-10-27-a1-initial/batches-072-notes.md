There are nine exercises, eighteen questions and fifty-four options. Every question has exactly three options. The full key sequence is A, B, C, A, B, C, B, C, A, B, C, A, C, A, B, C, A, B: six of each letter, with no three consecutive identical keys. All evidence appears verbatim in that joined text.

Scripts use short present-tense main clauses and simple commands. They contain no digits, dates, competing times, calculations, subordinate clauses, exception questions, corrections of a previously stated fact or third named person. The longest sentence is the nine-word question about the dog or bird. Competing objects have clear functions: a broken computer versus the phone used for work, a newspaper lying nearby versus the text each woman reads, or objects on a table versus socks inside a box. These distinctions must remain easy to hear after synthesis.

The visual hooks use concrete words such as `banaan`, `eend`, `ogen`, `hoed`, `bezem`, `oren`, `paraplu`, `bloem` and `kat`. `Bezem`, `paraplu`, `broden` and `bibliotheek` deserve particular attention during review. Each hook is visible in the context picture and is never the keyed listening fact. Learners need no cultural joke, idiom or new fictional-world rule to answer.

## Key and distractor audit

### `A1:listening:batch072-banaan-telefoon:1`

- q1, A, `Schrijf je naam op het papier.` explicitly supplies the information Roos must write. B, her address, and C, her number, are alternatives Roos asks about; Ruth answers `Alleen je naam.` Both wrong choices concern the same familiar act of writing personal information.
- q2, B, `Schrijf je naam op het papier.` explicitly supplies the writing surface. A, a book, is another object Roos has; the instruction does not ask her to write in it. C, her phone, comes from the banana-as-phone game; Ruth asks for paper. The brief hides all three candidate surfaces.

### `A1:listening:batch072-eend-reis:1`

- q1, C, `we nemen de trein.` states their transport. A, the bus, is Sabrina's question; Julio answers `Nee`. B, the bicycle, stays at home. The train choice is said directly and requires no route or timetable knowledge.
- q2, A, `Ik neem mijn tas.` states Julio's own luggage. B, his coat, and C, his book, transfer Sabrina's belongings to the wrong person: `Mijn jas en boek zijn klaar.` No third person's luggage or extra travel detail is introduced.

### `A1:listening:batch072-stoel-ogen:1`

- q1, B, `Ik zoek mijn pen.` names the lost object. A, her book, and C, her phone, are already present according to Ruben. They are nearby possessions, not the object Roos says she seeks.
- q2, C, `Je pen ligt onder de tafel.` names the pen's position. A, on the table, is the paper's position. B, next to the table, is the bag's position. The answer is a full phrase in one turn and needs no inference from the other placements.

### `A1:listening:batch072-grote-hoed:1`

- q1, B, `Ik koop brood.` gives Fatima's purchase. A, cake, and C, biscuit, are other foods Jerry has, not her order.
- q2, C, `Twee, graag.` gives the quantity in Fatima's reply to Jerry's offer of one, two or three. A and B repeat the two quantities she does not choose. The words are heard, with no price calculation or visual counting task.

### `A1:listening:batch072-bezem-feest:1`

- q1, A, `De radio, graag.` gives Daan's choice of music source. B, guitar, and C, piano, are the other two sources Roos offers. None is played or depicted before listening.
- q2, B, `Ik maak soep.` gives the food Daan prepares. A, bread, belongs to Roos's later turn. C, cake, is already ready; Daan does not say he makes it. The prompt uses the present-tense action `maakt`, matching the quoted statement.

### `A1:listening:batch072-doos-oren:1`

- q1, C, `Sokken.` directly answers Karim's question `Wat zit in de doos?`. A, apples, and B, a book, are explicitly on the table in the same short turn. Their position rules them out as contents of this box. An early author draft used `hier`; the final source names the table to prevent an ambiguous referent.
- q2, A, `Voor de kat.` directly answers Karim's question about the box's recipient. B, dog, and C, bird, are the animals he proposes. The cat's habit of sleeping in boxes makes the arrangement light and plausible; no pet-care knowledge is tested.

### `A1:listening:batch072-paraplu-werk:1`

- q1, C, `Ik wil koffie.` states Julio's drink. A, milk, and B, tea, are available nearby. They are alternatives, not the drink he requests.
- q2, A, `Ik werk met mijn telefoon.` states his work tool. B, his computer, is broken. C, a pen, is available with paper, but Julio says he works with his phone. The learner does not need to infer that he cannot work at all without a computer.

### `A1:listening:batch072-bloem-beker:1`

- q1, B, `Soep, graag.` gives Fatima's meal choice. A, bread, and C, rice, are other foods Jerry offers.
- q2, C, `Ik neem ook water.` gives her drink choice. A, milk, and B, juice, are other drinks Jerry mentions. The flower mug contains soil in the illustration and cannot supply the drink answer.

### `A1:listening:batch072-kat-boeken:1`

- q1, A, `Ik lees mijn mail.` is Ruth's own statement. B, a book, is what Roos reads. C, the newspaper, lies nearby, and neither speaker says she reads it. The statement contains no difficult paraphrase.
- q2, B, `Ik lees een boek.` is Roos's own statement. A, her mail, transfers Ruth's activity to the wrong speaker. C again selects the nearby newspaper. The two questions deliberately share short parallel options; remembering which woman speaks provides a small, explicit challenge.

## Doubts for independent review

- The banana is a pretend telephone during a stated classroom game. Check that the joke is understandable to adults without an idiom or a new rule they must learn.
- The box has socks inside but is intended as a cat bed. The second answer is explicit; check that this small oddity stays light and does not distract from the spoken facts.
- Check the baker's spoken quantity offer and Fatima's short `Twee, graag.` reply as audible beginner language. All three candidate counts occur in one simple offer.
- The library pair asks two related speaker-attribution questions. Check whether this small memory task stays within the requested easy target. No third speaker or similar names appear.
- Judge the two food-service exchanges for enough variety. They retain familiar food vocabulary while changing the task operation and setting.
- Rate simplicity, clarity, variety, visual interest and task/image fit separately from the editorial verdict. Text approval does not certify audio quality or the final rendered image.

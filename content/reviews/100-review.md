### A2:knm:batch033-schoolvakantie:1 (Vakantie buiten de schoolvakantie)

- tone: ordinary
- coherence_rating: 4
- situation_summary: Fatima, a mother in a hallway next to a packed suitcase and a wall calendar, cannot take time off in any school holiday because of her job. The question asks how long her daughter Amina may be away outside the school holidays.
- communicative_purpose: A parent in this position wants to know whether and how much extra leave a school can give. The learner leaves with the rule that the director may grant at most ten days, once per school year.
- contrivance_check: The picture (suitcase, calendar, mother and daughter at home) matches the trip being planned. The prompt itself sets up the condition the card depends on ("door haar werk ... in geen schoolvakantie weg"), so no condition is hidden. The card repeats the general ban first and then gives the exception, which is a natural order. Nothing needs an invented off-page event.
- unsupported_assumptions: (1) The prompt says "mag", but the card says the director "kan ... vrij geven": the leave is at the director's discretion, not an entitlement. The question is answerable as "maximum length", but the wording leaves a risk if an option says the leave is guaranteed or if "maximaal" is not reflected in the options. (2) The card says "dagen" and the prompt says "hoe lang"; the unit (schooldagen) and any limit on timing (e.g. first weeks of the school year) are not in the card. That is acceptable at A2 but Phase 2 must check the card against the current official rule and that no option depends on omitted detail. (3) "ouders" in the card against one mother in the prompt is harmless.
- picture/prompt fit: good. The picture does not show the answer (ten days is not visible; the calendar is stated to have no readable text).

### A2:knm:batch033-passief-kiesrecht:1 (Actief en passief kiesrecht)

- tone: ordinary
- coherence_rating: 4
- situation_summary: Sem, a teenager at a school desk, reads about "passief kiesrecht" in a textbook. The question asks what the term means.
- communicative_purpose: A resident meets the term in civic education or on a voting card and needs to know the difference between the right to vote and the right to be elected. The card gives both terms side by side with examples.
- contrivance_check: A schoolboy reading a textbook is a normal place to meet this term, and the picture (open textbook, classroom window) supports it without revealing the meaning. The term is quoted in the prompt, so the question is a plain definition question. No invented event is required.
- unsupported_assumptions: (1) The question is a vocabulary definition for a term that a newcomer may never have heard; it can only be answered from prior civic knowledge or by elimination. That is normal for KNM but means the options carry the whole load: Phase 2 must check that each wrong option is false as a statement about the Netherlands and not merely a wrong gloss of the term (blueprint §5, learner data on this item). (2) The card names the gemeenteraad and Tweede Kamer as examples; the prompt does not need them. (3) A teenage Sem is irrelevant to the rule (the age limit is not mentioned and should not be implied).
- picture/prompt fit: good; the textbook is closed to the reader and shows no text.

### A2:knm:batch032-vuurwerk:1 (Vuurwerk met Oud en Nieuw)

- tone: ordinary
- coherence_rating: 4
- situation_summary: Modibo stands on a balcony on a dark evening, looking over a row of terraced houses, with no fireworks in the picture. He wants to set off fireworks at Oud en Nieuw; the question asks what he may set off himself.
- communicative_purpose: A private person planning New Year's Eve wants to know which fireworks are legal for consumers. The card states the consumer rule and notes that associations have their own rules.
- contrivance_check: The evening balcony ties the picture to the New Year scene while holding back any fireworks, so the picture cannot reveal an option. "zelf" in the prompt marks Modibo as a private person, which is why the association remark in the card is not a competing answer. The question has one natural reading.

If the date or scope differs, the item must be revised or retired. (2) The sentence "Voor verenigingen gelden aparte regels" adds a clause the question does not need and a newcomer could read as a hint at an exception; the reviewer should consider whether it earns its place in a thirty-word card. (3) "klein vuurwerk" is not defined; the card's only example is "sterretjes", so options must stay consistent with that example. (4) Whether Modibo lives in a place with extra local limits is not set up and is not needed.
- picture/prompt fit: good; deliberately no fireworks, which avoids pre-answering.

- coherence_rating: 4

(2) "met rente" is an extra fact the question does not need; confirm that it is current and keep it only if it adds value. (3) The prompt says "Roos krijgt van DUO" while the card says "student", which is consistent.
- picture/prompt fit: good; neutral study scene.

### Per item

**Schoolvakantie (8.1.3).** Card 29 words, prompt 20, three options. Evidence ("Kunnen ouders ... weg? Dan kan de directeur maximaal tien dagen vrij geven") is verbatim in the card and entails the key. Rule 1: the prompt sets up the work condition. Rule 2: "Geen enkele dag" is false once the work condition holds; "Zo lang als Fatima zelf wil" is false (permission and ten-day cap). Rule 3: the question is "Hoe lang" and all three options are durations. Signals: key is the middle length (16/20/28 characters); no prompt word recurs only in the key; no "nooit/altijd". The author's doubt about "Maximaal tien dagen" as a hedged key: it is the natural form of a cap and the other two options are sharply different durations; I do not see it as a signal. Weakness: option C is easy to dismiss, so the item is mostly decided by the A-versus-B contrast; acceptable at A2. My phase-1 "mag vs kan": the page itself says "De directeur mag ... vrij geven" and "De directeur beslist", so "kan" in the card and "mag" in the prompt are both faithful. "Schooldagen": the law and the page say "dagen"; the card claims no unit, so no change. The explanation adds the first-two-weeks condition and the fine, both on the page.

**Passief kiesrecht (7.1.4).** Card 30 words, prompt 10. Evidence verbatim. Rule 2: the old "niet hoeven stemmen" is gone; "Het recht om de burgemeester te kiezen" is false about the Netherlands (benoeming by the Crown, Gemeentewet art. 61), so a learner cannot defend it as a true statement; "Het recht om te stemmen" is the definition of actief kiesrecht and so is the wrong answer to this prompt but a true description of that other term, which is intended (the item tests the contrast, and the prompt names the term). On the author's doubt about the burgemeester distractor: a learner who wrongly believes mayors are elected could choose it, but that belief is false and the item does not hinge on trivia, because the key can be found from the card or the prompt's term alone. Rule 1 and 3 do not apply to a pure definition. Signals: key is neither longest (24/38/26) nor echoing a prompt word only in the key (all options share "recht"). The author's decision to keep the definition form rather than turn it round is sound (the alternatives were weaker).

**Vuurwerk (2.1.5).** Card exactly 30 words, prompt 13. Evidence verbatim and entailing. Date verified (source 8, 9). The prompt asks what a consumer may set off, and the options (only small / none / also rockets after six) differ on exactly that (rule 3). Option C is false per Rijksoverheid and Stcrt. 2020 (no consumer rockets at all). Option B is false per the F1 rule. Answer-signal check: key 40 characters against 35 and 46, not longest; "Ook" in C is a normal marker for "more than small fireworks" and does not leak. The verenigingen sentence: it is not needed for the key but keeps "mogen zij niet" and "alleen" accurate for consumers and explains "zelf" in the prompt; it costs no option, so it helps slightly rather than hurts. It uses the last of the thirty words, so any future edit must remove something. Time-sensitive: re-check before each New Year.

**Basisbeurs (8.3.3).** Card exactly 30 words, prompt 17. Evidence verbatim and entailing. Rule 1: "Wanneer ..." asks for the condition. Rule 2: living with parents only changes the amount (DUO), and finding work after the studies is not a condition on the page (no such rule), so both distractors are false statements. Rule 3: three conditions in the same form. Signals: key 44 characters against 46 and 50; "binnen een jaar" in C mirrors the key's "binnen tien jaar" without giving the answer away. Author's doubt on "Wanneer" when other edge cases exist (stopping in the first year, illness or disability): those are not offered as options, and the card and key describe the general rule that the prompt asks for for a hbo basisbeurs; a learner who knows an edge case is not misled because none of the three options states it. The card says "hbo en universiteit" without excluding mbo 3 and 4 (also a prestatiebeurs per DUO); the prompt places Roos at the hbo, so nothing is claimed that is false.

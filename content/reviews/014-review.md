## Decisions on the coordinator's questions

**1. Gaps after a printed lead-in are acceptable and exam-like.** The official zinstaak is "complete one sentence inside a short e-mail or message", and its marking model names the structure that earns the grammar point (hoofdzin, bijzin, inversie, te + infinitief). A bijzin, an inversion or a te-infinitief cannot be forced by context alone; only a printed lead-in fixes them, which is why the author brief's own examples are lead-ins ("the gap follows omdat", "Ik probeer ___"). Downgrading the six lead-in tasks to `vrij` would remove the structure tests that tasks 1–8 exist for. The phrase "the gap is a whole sentence" in the review brief is read here as "the learner writes one whole clause or sentence, never a word or a phrase", which all eight tasks satisfy. Each lead-in was tested for whether it forces exactly the target:

| Lead-in | Target | Forced? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Daarom ___." (ziekmelding) | inversie | yes | a sentence-initial adverb takes the finite verb next; "Daarom, ik kan …" and "Daarom ik kan …" are the errors the task is meant to catch |
| ", omdat ___." (vacature) | bijzin | yes | subordinating conjunction, finite verb at the end |
| "laten weten of ___?" (examendatum) | bijzin | yes | "laten weten" needs a complement, so "of" can only be the complementizer of an indirect question (the coordinating "of" = "or" reading needs a comma and a second main clause, which the body does not offer) |
| "lukt het mij niet om ___." (groepsverslag) | te-infinitief | yes | "om" after "lukt het … niet" takes "… te + infinitief" and nothing else |
| ", want ___." (verjaardag) | hoofdzin | yes | coordinating conjunction, main-clause order; the deliberate counterpart of "omdat" in vacature |
| "Misschien ___?" (sportschool) | inversie | yes | sentence-initial adverb; the colloquial "Misschien dat we …?" has no inversion and is not the target (the marking model names the structure, so it earns no grammar point; the feedback should say so) |

**2. The two prompt wordings are consistent and clear.** "Maak de e-mail af. Maak de zin op de open plek af." is used for the six lead-in gaps (the sentence has begun and the learner finishes it) and "Maak de e-mail af. Schrijf één zin op de open plek." for the two open gaps (a whole sentence is missing). Each prompt matches its gap type; one wording for both would either invite a second capitalised sentence after "Daarom" or misdescribe the open gaps. The rule should be written into blueprint §4.8 and the author brief, which currently show only the "Schrijf één zin" form (coordinator note).

**3. "of" as an indirect-question marker is a fair bijzin test at B1.** "Kunt u mij laten weten of …", "Ik wil graag weten of …" and "Ik twijfel of …" are core B1 e-mail formulae (the batch's own studieadviseur body uses one), the verb-final rule is the same as after "dat" and "omdat", and main-clause order inside an of-clause is a frequent learner error. Two bijzin tasks with two different subordinators (omdat, of) spread the test better than omdat plus dat. The author's fallback "Ik hoop dat ___" is not needed.

### 7. `B1:writing:batch014-verjaardag:1` — revise (one criterion)

Lead-in "Ik wilde echt komen, maar het lukte niet, want ___." Scaffold: mila@mail.nl, subject "Sorry voor gisteren", "Hoi Mila," / "Groetjes,"; "je" throughout. Body: "Sorry dat ik gisteren niet op je verjaardag was. Ik wilde echt komen, maar het lukte niet, want ___. Ik hoop dat je toch een gezellige avond hebt gehad. Zaterdag kom ik langs met je cadeau." The apology and "het lukte niet" leave only the cause of the absence; "want" forces main-clause order, the deliberate contrast with vacature's "omdat". Sample "ik wilde heel graag naar de feest komen": correct main clause (quoted whole), no cause (null, it repeats the wish and is incoherent after "het lukte niet", a plausible learner slip); "de feest" (gender, tolerated). Model "mijn trein stond door een storing twee uur stil bij Utrecht" → "…, want mijn trein stond door een storing twee uur stil bij Utrecht." (11 typed words): a concrete cause, subject then verb. **Alternative:** "…, want mijn dochter werd 's middags ziek en ik kon geen oppas vinden." Also correct, and the reason for the revision: "…, want gisteren moest ik onverwacht tot tien uur werken." (inversion after a fronted adverb inside the want-clause).

The task, sample, model and starters are sound. Criterion 2 is not: "De zin is een correcte hoofdzin na 'want': eerst het onderwerp, dan de persoonsvorm (geen bijzinsvolgorde)." / "…: first the subject, then the finite verb (no subordinate-clause order)." After "want" any main-clause order is correct, including a fronted adverbial with inversion ("want gisteren was ik ziek", "want 's avonds moest ik werken", "want opeens kreeg mijn zoon koorts"), which learners produce often. The criterion is shown to the learner as a self-check and sent to the feedback judge; as worded it fails those correct sentences (rubric 4, 5). The official grammar point for a want-task is a correct hoofdzin, not subject-first order. Replace the pair with:

- nl: `De zin is een correcte hoofdzin na 'want': de persoonsvorm staat op de tweede plaats (hoofdzinsvolgorde), niet achteraan zoals in een bijzin.`
- en: `The sentence is a correct main clause after 'want': the finite verb is in second position (main-clause order), not at the end as in a subordinate clause.`

The `adequacyNote`, sample, quotes and model stay as they are; the starters ("ik moest … tot …", "er was een probleem met …") remain valid under the new wording. Level: comparable. Originality: a birthday is on the needed list; the only birthday item is the A2 speaking picture *batch009-verjaardag* (a child's party), another part, level and act; *oppassen* and *plant* (A2 writing) ask a friend a favour. The catalogue title "Sorry voor de verjaardag" reads as an apology for the birthday itself; "Verjaardag gemist" is suggested below.

## 3. Decisions on the author's doubts (notes §7)

1. **Domain tags** → ophalen `overig` (E1); werkschoenen stays `werk`: safety shoes for a warehouse job, the urgency is the new job and the vocabulary is work vocabulary; a boundary case, recorded as such. Counts werk 3 / educatie 1 / overig 4 instead of the requested 3 / 2 / 3; running total with batch 014: werk 6, educatie 4, overig 6.
2. **dienstruil sample** → keep. "als jij wilt dat ik jouw avonddienst overneem" is a correct als-clause (verb-final "wilt", "overneem" at the end of the dat-clause) that paraphrases the body's first sentence and names no condition of the writer's own; it is the copied-prompt failure the exam rules describe, and it leaves "Dan geef ik de ruil meteen door" without a ruil. It earns 0 for adequacy; the feedback should name it as a repetition of the request, not as an odd sentence.
3. **werkschoenen versus 015-kledingkast** → distinct: a zinstaak naming a wrong size in one sentence against a korte schrijftaak about damage and late delivery. Keep them apart if a mixed set is ever built; a form never has both at the zinstaak position.
4. **"…, maar ___." as the hoofdzin trigger** → accepted. "maar" coordinates like "want" (014-verjaardag); the criterion carries the second-position wording the 014 review required, so an inverted main clause after "maar" ("maar helaas zit er maat 41 in de doos") earns the point and verb-third or verb-final order fails it. One property to tell the feedback judge: a verbless continuation ("maar helaas in de verkeerde maat") is natural Dutch and adequate, but shows no hoofdzin, so it earns no grammar point; the learner should be told to write a full clause, not that the phrase is wrong.
5. **"Volgens het huurcontract ___." as the inversion trigger** → accepted. A fronted prepositional phrase forces the finite verb into second position exactly as "Daarom" does, no correct completion begins with the subject, and it is the phrase learners write in such e-mails; the sample shows the error the task exists for. Same feedback note as 4 for a verbless completion ("Volgens het huurcontract binnen twee weken").
6. **"als" read as "when"** → either reading is verb-final; "Dat wil ik wel doen, als" makes the condition reading the natural one, and criterion 1 asks for a condition. Keep.
7. **Body length** → keep. 38–54 words in four or five sentences is still a short e-mail; the 22-word sentence in ophalen is two coordinated main clauses with "dus" and stays plain B1 (§10: none over 30). Trimming a fact would weaken the time chain that forces the request.
8. **afvalpas sample** → keep: the off-topic failure (registration at the gemeente) is a real learner move and leaves "Zonder de pas" without a referent.
9. **Two moves in one batch** → accepted (both settings were fixed by the coordinator); borg is moving out, afvalpas moving in, and no third task carries the motif. Placed in different drills below.
10. **"Groet," for the manager** → keep; a realistic closing to a manager on first-name terms and one more closing form in the bank.
11. **No second-person form in the ophalen body** → fine; salutation, closing and the model's "bij u" fix the register (as 014-sportschool).
12. **Deposit term** → the model's two weeks is the statutory term (Wet goed verhuurderschap: 14 days, 30 when costs are deducted; Rijksoverheid page re-checked 11 September 2026); the criterion does not require the term, so "binnen een maand" is adequate too. The e-mail is undated; "nog niet teruggekregen" holds whether or not the 14 days have passed.
13. **Slugs and concurrent batches** → confirmed: none of the eight slugs exists in any part of the catalogue or in batches 026–031; the checker gives no slug warning. Cross-part echoes recorded in section 5.
14. **Wilgendam** → accepted under the coordinator's rule: the web search finds no municipality, only a street in Noordwijk and a farm in Belgium; the name occurs only in the `to` address.

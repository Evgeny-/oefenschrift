# Level check: batch 009 (A2 Spreken)

Independent level check (blueprint section 12, step 5) of the 24 A2 speaking tasks in `content/batches/009-original.json` (SHA-256 `c02550323cadd0e2e3667c858170ae085f36fc278b5d567e5f4986ac99e1370c`), 10 September 2026. The catalogue copies differ from the batch file only in `status`, `revision`, `promptAudio`, `cueAudio` and the image `file` fields (checked field by field), so the text judged here is the text learners get.

## Method

1. Read before judging: `content/blueprint.md` sections 2, 4.3, 5 and 10; `docs/research/exam-blueprints-2026-09-10.md` section 3.1 "Spreken A2" (with section 4 on scoring and the speaking rules in 6.5); `content/reviews/rubric.md` checks 1–2; the format of `content/reviews/008-level-check.md`. Not read: `content/reviews/009-review.*` and `content/batches/009-notes.md`.
2. Yardstick: the official task as described in 3.1 — 16 tasks in 35 minutes, four onderdelen of four: (1) a person on video asks you something directly and you answer; (2) one picture: describe and give an opinion, "Sem luncht op school. Vertel wat Sem kan eten. Vertel ook wat u van dit eten vindt. Gebruik het plaatje."; (3) two pictures: choose and explain, "In welk gebouw hebt u liever les? Vertel ook waarom. Kies een van de plaatjes."; (4) three pictures: narrate, "Kijk naar de plaatjes. Vertel wat Ricardo doet. Vertel iets over alle plaatjes." Instructions printed and read aloud; raters score task fulfilment and content, comprehensibility, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation. Section 6.5 adds the checklist the type implies: both parts of a two-part question, both pictures mentioned, all three pictures covered; blueprint 4.3: two parts answered; a choice and a reason; all three pictures mentioned. `content/exemplars/` (section 10) does not exist, so there was no exemplar of our own to compare with.
3. Calibration items: `A2:speaking:batch001-apotheek:1`, `A2:speaking:batch002-kapper:1`, `A2:speaking:batch003-monteur:1` (and the six others of batches 001–003). These are the pre-blueprint "phone someone and say three things" format: no task type, no cue, no picture, no timing, models of 13–22 words. They sit below the official demand and were used as a lower bound only.
4. Per task: prompt level; the cue (video answer: natural speech, one two-part question, length in words and seconds from the audio manifest); what the criteria demand against the official scoring; the model (25–45 words, A2 grammar, every criterion met); whether each picture set carries the task without text. Heuristics from section 10 were computed for every prompt, cue and model: word and sentence counts, average and longest sentence, subordinators (only omdat, als, dat, wanneer, toen allowed), tense, explicit numbers and times.
5. All 42 pictures were looked at in `assets/images/` (the catalogue `file` fields), since in three of the four types the picture is the task.
6. Three display facts shape the verdicts. `app/components/OpenExercise.tsx` never prints the cue text (the clip button says only "U hoort: Julio"), so the learner has to understand the spoken question — good. It does print the criteria as a bullet list under the prompt before the learner speaks, for all four speaking types (the hide-before-answering rule covers only picture-note, zinstaak and deelschrijftaak). The feedback judge (`server/services.ts`) receives the prompt and the Dutch criteria, not the cue text, and marks every criterion met or not met, so the criteria are the scoring content. The line "10 seconden voorbereiden · 40 seconden spreektijd" is informational; the recorder (`SpeakingRecorder.tsx`) runs to 2:00 and clips can be replayed. Sentence starters (`009-starters.json`) are collapsed and were left out.
7. Verdict scale: easier / comparable / harder than the official task of the same type. Flags: too easy, too hard, off-level, off-format, none.

### Item notes

- **ochtenddienst.** Situation line tells why the question comes ("Een collega maakt het rooster"). Cue: informal "jij" from a colleague, a preference question plus "En waarom?" — choice and reason, both A2 speech acts. Model: als-clause with verb-final order, "om zeven uur"; "fris" the only word below the top band. Comparable in content; see the flagged finding.
- **thuis-oefenen.** Cue: the docent's "u" is plausible in adult education; how + how often, both concrete. Model: frequency phrase "bijna elke dag, een half uur"; "versta" is a learner's daily verb. Comparable in content.
- **nieuwe-buurvrouw.** The longest cue sentence (15 words) but two coordinated everyday questions (hoe lang, wat vindt u van). Model: six sentences of at most 8 words, the plainest of the batch; "Alleen parkeren is soms moeilijk" gives the opinion two sides. Comparable in content; the natural opener of the type.
- **slaap.** Cue: "U zegt dat u vaak moe bent" motivates two clock-time questions — the A2 "hoe laat" skill exactly. Model: "om twaalf uur", "om zes uur", "maar zes uur slaap", separable "word … wakker" in a main clause. Comparable in content.
- **abonnement.** Cue: two short questions in two sentences (a choice, a yes/no), the easiest to hold in memory; "abonnement" and "prepaidkaart" are the words a phone shop uses. Model: one 14-word want-sentence; ends with a question back ("Wat kost een abonnement per maand?"), a natural A2 move. Comparable in content.
- **bushalte.** Cue: the learner answers as a local and must invent a line and a frequency — the only cue asking for information rather than a personal fact or opinion, the same imaginative step the picture-choose tasks ask for. Model 27 words (shortest): "Lijn vier", "elk kwartier", "aan de overkant", "ongeveer tien minuten". Comparable in content; the most demanding of the six because a frequency phrase is produced less often than a clock time.
- **schoonmaak.** "schoonmaakbedrijf" is transparent from "maakt … schoon". Picture: mop in a bucket, vacuum cleaner, spray bottle, cloth, desk and chair — five objects, no text; two A2 household words (emmer, stofzuiger, doek, fles) satisfy the criterion; "dweil" and "spuitfles" appear only in the model. Opinion "van dit werk". Comparable.
- **gymles.** Picture: a boy on a rope (green sweater = Sem), two pupils throwing a ball, a girl jumping by a mat — three distinct actions (klimmen, gooien, springen; touw, bal, mat). Opinion "sport op school". Model: six sentences of at most 9 words. Comparable.
- **afval.** Picture: green container with a round opening, blue with a slot, grey; Karim holds a bag of bottles and flattened cardboard — the two kinds of waste are unmistakable. ## Flagged tasks

### ochtenddienst, thuis-oefenen, nieuwe-buurvrouw, slaap, abonnement, bushalte — easier as displayed, too easy (display)

The content of these six is on level and exam-like: a one-line situation, the official "U hoort de vraag. Geef antwoord.", a still of the speaker facing the learner, and a spoken cue of 16–21 words (5.5–7.0 s) in natural Dutch with the right register (jij from a colleague, u from a teacher, doctor, neighbour, shop employee and stranger). The cue is heard, not read — the app prints only "U hoort: Julio". Each cue is one two-part question, as the research checklist expects, and both parts are A2 speech acts (a preference and a reason, how and how often, how long and an opinion, two clock times, a choice and a yes/no, a bus line and a frequency). The models (27–36 words, four to six sentences, at most one als-clause) answer both parts and add one detail.

What makes them easier than the official task is the display. `OpenExercise.tsx` prints the criteria under the prompt before the learner records, and for this type the criteria are a written paraphrase of the spoken question: "Zeg of u liever in de ochtend of in de avond werkt. Zeg waarom." / "Zeg hoe laat u naar bed gaat. Zeg hoe laat u opstaat." / "Zeg welke bus naar het ziekenhuis gaat. Zeg hoe vaak de bus rijdt." A learner who did not understand the audio can answer from the list, and the replayable clip removes what is left of the listening load. In the official onderdeel the candidate has only the person on video; understanding the question is half the task.

What would make them on-level: a display rule, not a content change — for `taskType: video-answer` do not show the requirements list before speaking (extend the rule in `OpenExercise.tsx` that already hides it for picture-note, zinstaak and deelschrijftaak), and show it in the self-check and feedback stage, where rubric check 8 wants it. The judge is unaffected: it receives the criteria text either way. If a visible list before speaking is wanted, the content-preserving alternative is a neutral per-criterion display text ("Geef antwoord op het eerste deel van de vraag." / "Geef antwoord op het tweede deel." / "Geef nog een detail.") with the specific text kept for the judge; that needs a new field. In mock mode the cue should also play once. After the display fix all six are comparable.

## Notes that do not change a verdict

- **Third criterion in the video-answer tasks.** "Geef nog een detail, bijvoorbeeld een tijd." / "Geef een reden of een detail." / "Geef nog een detail over uw slaap." is not in the official demand (research 6.5: both parts of a two-part question; blueprint 4.3: two parts answered; the answer is "one or two sentences"). Because the judge scores every criterion, an answer that would satisfy the official rater in two sentences is reported as two of three. It is a mild extension that fills the 40 seconds and does no harm in the exam; if exact official scoring is wanted, drop the third criterion from the six video-answer tasks (the starters for those tasks then go through the re-review of section 12.8). I would leave it and let the coordinator decide.
- **Third criterion in the picture-choose tasks.** "Zeg ook iets over het andere plaatje." follows the research checklist ("both pictures mentioned") but goes beyond blueprint 4.3 ("a choice and a reason"). The two documents should say the same thing; the tasks follow the research. No change requested.
- **Criteria 1 and 2 of the describe tasks overlap** ("Vertel wat u op het plaatje ziet" and "Noem minstens twee dingen …"): a learner who names two things has done both. Harmless; "minstens twee" is a sensible operationalisation of "Gebruik het plaatje" and none of the criteria gives away a word the picture asks for, so printing them before speaking is fine for this type, as it is for choose and sequence, where the criteria only restate the prompt.
- **Pictures.** All 42 carry no readable text (a bus pictogram, a blank badge, a blank roof sign and a blank number plate are the only signs); left-hand drive, the pink licence, the yellow plate, the three-container row and the gemeente procedure are right for the Netherlands; every describe picture offers at least four nameable objects or actions, every pair differs in the one thing asked, every sequence has three distinct actions. Continuity slips for the media reviewer, none of which changes what the learner says: rijbewijs panel 2 (instructor and car changed — the one case where the sequence rule "regenerate when a trait changes" arguably applies), vakantie panel 2 (suitcase colour), alleen-samen picture 2 and paspoort panels 1–2 (featureless faces), Fatima's scarf colour across her six pictures (black in the train, grey-green elsewhere).
- **ehbo** could say "Vertel wat Roos in de doos heeft." to match a picture where the objects lie in front of the box; optional.
- **sportschool-park** model: the 16-word sentence is at the limit; "In een sportschool, zoals op het eerste plaatje, is het vaak druk. En je moet betalen." is the A2 version. Optional.
- **Samples** are correct Dutch that miss one criterion each (the `null` in `quotes`); unlike the writing batch they carry no learner errors. For speaking the transcript supplies the errors, so this is fine.
- **Timing.** The app announces "10 seconden voorbereiden · 40 seconden spreektijd" but does not enforce either; the recorder allows 2:00 and clips replay. This is the mock-mode gap the blueprint already names (4.7), not a batch matter, but with the printed criteria it is what makes the video-answer type easy today.
- **Calibration.** Against batches 001–003 (13–22-word models, no cue, no picture, no timing) this batch is a different exam part in all but name; those nine items are the warm-up layer the research (section 7) already proposes to retire from A2.

## Conclusion

### A fair A2 speaking drill

All 24 tasks are comparable to the official task of their type in content; six (the video answers) are easier only because of how the app displays them; none is off-level or off-format. Every prompt uses the official instruction pattern, every picture set carries its task without text, every cue is natural spoken Dutch of one two-part question, and every model is A2 Dutch of 27–45 words that meets its criteria. Difficulty sits where the official exam puts it — in finding the words for what the picture shows and in giving a reason — not in the prompt.

Coverage: 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 across all eight domains (werk, opleiding, wonen-buurt, gezondheid, winkels-diensten, instanties, vervoer, vrije-tijd-familie), no setting repeated from the section 9 list. That is one full mock form (4 + 4 + 4 + 4) with two spare tasks per type; a second mock without shared tasks needs eight per type.

### Sets

`practice-sets.json` groups the batch as eight drills of three (a2-speaking-04 … 11), one type per drill. Blueprint section 11 and research 6.3 define the A2 speaking drill as one onderdeel of four tasks. With six per type, three-and-three is the even split; the alternative is one onderdeel of four plus a short drill of two per type, or waiting for the next batch of this part to make two onderdelen of four. Order within a type, easiest first:

- Video answer: nieuwe-buurvrouw → slaap → thuis-oefenen → ochtenddienst → abonnement → bushalte.
- One picture: verjaardag → kringloop → gymles → afval → schoonmaak → ehbo.
- Two pictures: klas-thuis → sportschool-park → trein-auto → flat-dorp → alleen-samen → gemeente-bellen.
- Three pictures: vakantie → bioscoop → kleermaker → paspoort → brief-gemeente → rijbewijs.

The current sets do not follow this order (a2-speaking-04 opens with ochtenddienst, a2-speaking-10 with paspoort); re-ordering is a set edit, not a content edit.

### Recommended actions

1. **Video answers** (coordinator/app): hide the requirements list before speaking for `taskType: video-answer`; show it in the review stage; play the cue once in mock mode. No content change.
2. **Video answers, criterion 3** (coordinator decides): keep as a speaking-time extension, or drop it for exact official scoring (starters re-review follows).
3. **Pictures** (media reviewer): rijbewijs panel 2 is the one to regenerate under the sequence rule; vakantie panel 2, alleen-samen picture 2 and paspoort panels 1–2 are cosmetic.
4. **Blueprint 4.3 wording** (coordinator): align "a choice and a reason" with the research checklist "both pictures mentioned", or the other way round, so the next author has one rule.
5. Optional wording: ehbo prompt, sportschool-park model sentence, drill order above.

## Limitations

- No official exemplar is in the repository and none was consulted directly; the yardstick is the research document's description of the official onderdelen (verified by the coordinator on 10 September 2026), and its example prompts are the only official wording I compared against. The length of an official video question (one part or two) is not recorded there; the two-part form follows the research checklist.
- The official rating scales for A2 Spreken are named in the research document but not quantified (DUO publishes no cesuur), so "criteria against official scoring" means against the task-fulfilment checklist, not against point values.
- I read the cues and instructions and took their durations and round-trip figures from the audio manifest; I did not listen to the clips, so pace, intonation and the naturalness of the delivery are not judged here.
- Video is substituted by audio plus a still throughout, as the blueprint prescribes; whether the still gives less context than the official video is a media question, not a level question.
- Level is an authoring target; this check is one reviewer's reading against a description of the exam, not a validation with learners or teachers. `targetLevelValidated` stays false.

# Batch 026: design notes (A2 Spreken, twelve tasks)

Batch 026 contains twelve original A2 speaking tasks in the four task types of the DUO "Spreken A2" exam (16 tasks in 35 minutes, no multiple choice since 1 March 2025) as described in `content/blueprint.md` §4.3 and `docs/research/exam-blueprints-2026-09-10.md` §3.1 "Spreken A2": three `video-answer` tasks (a character asks one two-part question; the learner answers), three `picture-describe` tasks (one picture: describe and give an opinion), three `picture-choose` tasks (two pictures: choose, say why, mention the other picture) and three `picture-sequence` tasks (three pictures of one cast member: narrate all three). It is the second batch in this shape after batch 009 (integrated) and runs in parallel with batch 027, which covers a sick-day call, a language-café evening, an evening course, a waiting room, a kitchen, a supermarket queue, morning or evening work, cash or card, bus or bicycle, a job interview, moving house and a dentist visit; none of those settings is used here.

Every situation, cue, criterion, sample, quote, model and image brief was written for this project. Official material was used for structure only: the instruction sentences ("U hoort de vraag. Geef antwoord.", "Vertel wat … Vertel ook wat u van … vindt. Gebruik het plaatje.", "… liever …? Vertel ook waarom. Kies een van de plaatjes.", "Kijk naar de plaatjes. Vertel wat … doet. Vertel iets over alle plaatjes.") follow the official patterns as batch 009 recorded them; no official scenario, picture or question is reused (the official examples are a school lunch, a shop or building choice and a hairdresser).

Level labels are authoring targets (`targetLevelValidated: false`). Nothing here claims official equivalence or a pass prediction. The rater scales live in the `a2-spreken` rubric; the criteria only carry the content checks.

## Conventions (as in batch 009, with the differences named)

- Fields: `id`, `level`, `part`, `exam: duo-a2`, `taskType`, `domain`, `title`, `status: draft`, `targetLevelValidated: false`, `rubric: a2-spreken`, `speakingSeconds: 40`, `prepSeconds: 10`, `prompt`, `cue` (video-answer only: `{speaker, role, text}` with a role from `config/voices.json`), `images` (`{brief, alt, kind: drawing, size?}`: `still` for the video-answer still, `pair` for the two choice pictures, `sequence` for the three panels, no `size` for the one-picture description so `scripts/illustrate.ts` takes `single`), `criteria` (`[[nl, en], …]`), `sample`, `quotes`, `model`.
- Three criteria per task, mirroring the type exactly as the 009 review confirmed (its answers to doubts 2–4): video answer = part one of the cue, part two, one more detail or reason; describe = the scene, at least two of the things or actions asked for, the opinion; choose = the choice, a reason, something about the other picture; sequence = one criterion per picture in order.
- Video-answer prompts use the composite pattern of the integrated batch 009: "<situatie>. <Persoon> stelt u een vraag. U hoort de vraag. Geef antwoord." (blueprint §4.3 still prints "U hoort een vraag"; see doubt 1). Cues are 18–20 words, one two-part question each, `je` from a colleague and a classmate, `u` from the doctor's assistant; numbers in words ("twaalf uur"); no cue asks for a name, address, date of birth or other identifying data. Non-cast speakers are labelled in lower case with the article ("de assistente"), as the 009 review asked, so the app caption reads "U hoort: de assistente".
- **Samples carry one or two typical learner errors each** (`docs/briefs/author.md`: "with one or two typical learner errors"), unlike batch 009, whose samples were error-free (accepted there because the 009 checklist did not ask for errors). The errors are listed per task in §2 so the reviewer can see they are deliberate. Every sample still fulfils all criteria but one (that quote is `null`); quotes are exact substrings of the sample, errors included. Null positions across the batch: criterion 1 three times, criterion 2 five times, criterion 3 four times.
- Models are 39–44 words of spoken A2 Dutch in five to seven main clauses (average 6.3–8.6 words per sentence, longest 13), subordinate clauses only with `dat` ("Dan weet ik zeker dat de maat goed is") and `want`/`maar`/`en` coordination, present tense with one perfect ("heb ik het woord gordijn geleerd"), times and numbers explicit. No prompt sentence appears in a sample or model (the regen model says "Ik zie een bushalte in de regen" rather than repeating the prompt's "Het regent.").
- Cast from `config/illustration.json`, named in the briefs exactly as in the config with the trait string verbatim in parentheses, and always mid-sentence for `meneer De Vries` and `mevrouw Bakker` because `expandCast` matches the lower-case form: Roos (still), Modibo (still and the library sequence), Hasan, Sabrina, Amina, meneer De Vries (pair), Julio (pair), Karim (pair), Sem (sequence), Fatima and mevrouw Bakker (sequence). Every non-cast figure that recurs across pictures has three fixed traits repeated verbatim: the two friends in the feest-thuis pair, the librarian in bibliotheekboek panels 2 and 3. One-off figures (the stallholder, the older woman on the bench, the mother at the gate, the shop assistant, the waiter) carry one or two traits. Recurring objects are named the same way in every picture (Sem's small backpack, Julio's bag over his shoulder, the blue shirt, the pot with a lid, the thick book).
- Image briefs stay within the house style ("a few simple shapes"): four to eight nameable shapes per picture, no text objects at all (no calendars, clocks, signs, screens with content, price tags, menus, house numbers, book titles: every screen, card and cover is declared blank), and a "No text" clause in every brief. The three describe pictures each offer three or four nameable actions or objects so "minstens twee" leaves slack.
- Alt texts are neutral Dutch descriptions; for describe tasks they name the pictured things (a screen-reader user needs the content to answer an open task); for choice tasks they describe each option without preferring one.
- No organisation, shop, firm, street or web address is invented anywhere in the batch (the library, the practice, the market and the restaurant are unnamed), so the web check of blueprint §13 has nothing to check.

## 2. Per task: casting, the omitted criterion, sample errors, how the model meets every criterion

Word counts are the checker's (whitespace tokens).

### Video-answer

1. **lunchpauze** (`A2:speaking:batch026-lunchpauze:1`). Cue: Roos, a young colleague, informal `jij/je`, role `f-young` (Roos in the voices file: "young colleague"); 18 words, opener "Hé, het is bijna twaalf uur", then "Wat eet jij meestal in de lunchpauze, en waar eet je dan?". Still: Roos at a desk with a laptop (blank screen) and a closed lunch box, turned to the viewer. Sample (28 words) omits criterion 2: it says what she eats and with whom, "Ik neem mijn eten mee van thuis" is where the food comes from, not where she eats. Errors: missing article ("meestal broodje met kaas"), wrong gender ("over de weekend"). Model (39 words): broodje met kaas en een appel (1); "Ik eet in de kantine, aan een grote tafel" (2); two colleagues, a half-hour break, a walk outside (3).
2. **woorden-leren**. Cue: Modibo, a classmate, `jij/je`, role `m-young` (Nick: "student"); 19 words; "Hoe onthoud jij nieuwe woorden? En welk woord heb je deze week geleerd?". Still: Modibo at a classroom table with a closed notebook and a pen. Sample (25 words) omits criterion 2: a method and why it helps, but no word is named. Errors: no inversion after a fronted adverbial ("Elke avond ik lees ze"), verb form ("Dat help goed"). Model (43 words): cards and notes on objects (1); "Deze week heb ik het woord gordijn geleerd" (2); its meaning ("Dat hangt voor het raam") and a tip ("Zo onthoud ik het beter") (3). Any word the learner names satisfies criterion 2.
3. **huisarts-afspraak**. Cue: the doctor's assistant at the desk, `u`, role `f-adult` (Esmee: "huisarts assistant" is listed under this role); 20 words; "Wilt u een afspraak in de ochtend of in de middag? En kunt u kort zeggen waarvoor u komt?" (Dutch assistants ask the reason for the visit to plan the appointment; the learner may invent any mild reason). Still: a non-cast assistant with three traits (about 40, short blond hair, dark blue blouse) behind the reception desk with a telephone; no screen, no white coat. Sample (23 words) omits criterion 1: a reason (back pain when sitting), a day, but never morning or afternoon. Errors: verb-final order dropped in the subclause ("als ik zit lang"), no inversion ("Dan ik ben vrij"). Model (39 words): "een afspraak in de middag, want 's ochtends werk ik" (1); a cough and a sore throat (2); "morgen om drie uur", "Woensdag kan ik ook" (3). Nothing graphic: a cough, a sore throat, a back that hurts when sitting.

### Picture-describe

4. **marktkraam**. Prompt: "Hasan doet boodschappen op de markt. Vertel wat Hasan bij deze kraam kan kopen. Vertel ook wat u van de markt vindt. Gebruik het plaatje." Picture: Hasan before a stall with a striped awning; apples, tomatoes, a bunch of carrots; a woman in a green apron holds out a paper bag (no price tags). Sample (20 words) omits criterion 3 (no opinion). Errors: infinitive before the objects ("Hij kan kopen appels en tomaten"), verb form ("De vrouw geef hem"). Model (40 words): Hasan at a stall on the market (1); appels, tomaten, wortels (2); "gezellig", fresh fruit, often cheaper than the supermarket (3).
5. **regen**. Prompt: "Sabrina wacht op de bus. Het regent. Vertel wat de mensen bij de halte doen. Vertel ook wat u van reizen met de bus vindt. Gebruik het plaatje." Picture: Sabrina under a yellow umbrella in the rain, an older woman on the bench under the shelter roof, a bus approaching; no sign on the stop, no number on the bus. Sample (24 words) omits criterion 3 (no opinion) and names two actions (standing with an umbrella and waiting, sitting on the bench). Errors: "Het regent veel" (lexical, for "hard"), "op de bank" (sofa) for "op het bankje". Model (44 words): a bus stop in the rain (1); Sabrina waits under an umbrella, the older woman sits on the bench, the bus is coming (2); "prima", cheap but sometimes late (3).
6. **schoolplein**. Prompt: "De school is uit. Amina is nog op het schoolplein. Vertel wat de kinderen doen. Vertel ook wat u van buiten spelen vindt. Gebruik het plaatje." Picture: a playground with a low fence and an open gate; Amina skips, two children kick a ball, a mother with a bicycle waits at the gate (the mother makes "de school is uit" visible without a clock). Sample (23 words) omits criterion 2: one action only ("Amina springt met een touw"). Errors: plural ("kinders"), agreement ("ze beweegt"). Model (44 words): a schoolyard (1); skipping, playing with a ball, and the mother at the gate as a third element (2); good for children, movement and fresh air, "Binnen zitten ze vaak achter een scherm" (3). No sentence assumes the learner has children.

### Picture-choose

7. **online-winkel**. Prompt: "U wilt nieuwe kleren kopen. Koopt u liever online of in een winkel? Vertel ook waarom. Kies een van de plaatjes." 9. **feest-thuis**. Prompt: "U bent geslaagd voor uw examen. U wilt dat vieren met vrienden. Viert u het liever thuis of in een restaurant? Vertel ook waarom. Kies een van de plaatjes." Errors: analytic comparative ("meer leuk"), no inversion after the fronted phrase ("Thuis, op het eerste plaatje, ze zitten"). Model (39 words): at home, picture one (1); quiet, staying long, cooking for friends (2); "Een restaurant, zoals op het tweede plaatje, is duur en vaak druk" (3).

12. **koken-buurvrouw**. Prompt: "Fatima kookt voor haar buurvrouw, mevrouw Bakker. Kijk naar de plaatjes. Vertel wat Fatima doet. Vertel iets over alle plaatjes." The reason for the meal is left open; any invented reason is acceptable.

1. **Video-answer prompt sentence.** The batch uses 009's integrated pattern "… stelt u een vraag. U hoort de vraag. Geef antwoord." while blueprint §4.3 prints "U hoort een vraag. Geef antwoord." The 009 review left the choice to the coordinator and no decision is recorded in the blueprint; changing the three prompts to the blueprint's sentence is a byte edit with no other effect.
2. **woorden-leren versus 009 thuis-oefenen.** Both are questions about learning Dutch from a person at the language school. The acts differ (a memory method plus a concrete word, versus practice habits plus frequency), the speaker differs (a classmate with `je`, versus the teacher with `u`), and the coordinator asked for this subject; if the reviewer reads it as the same setting, the alternative cue for Modibo is "Hé, ik heb gisteren de woordentoets gemist. Welke woorden waren moeilijk, en hoe heb jij ze geleerd?" (same criteria, order swapped).

If the shared setting is judged a repeat, the same brief works on a station platform ("Sabrina wacht op de trein") with the third criterion "wat u van reizen met de trein vindt" and the model's "bus" replaced by "trein"; the picture then loses the shelter bench.

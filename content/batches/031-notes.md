# Batch 031: design notes (B1 Spreken, eight middellange taken)

Batch 031 contains eight original middellange spreektaken (`taskType: middellang`) for Deel 2 of Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I Spreken, written against `content/blueprint.md` §4.9 (field shapes), §6 (voice roles), §7 (illustrations), §8–10 and the "Spreken I" paragraph of `docs/research/exam-blueprints-2026-09-10.md`: 15 seconds of preparation and 30 seconds of speaking, a spoken cue or none, one to three pictures or a table, and one of the Deel 2 speech acts (narrate three pictures, convince with two reasons, advise with at least two points, instruct step by step, compare two options and choose). Batch 017 (integrated, reviewed in `content/reviews/017-review.md`) is the format example; its reviewer decisions were applied here from the start: no criterion checks tense or connectors, no sample or model opens with the prompt's clause, sequences keep one cast member with verbatim traits and a clear order, the pair differs only in the step asked, tables are four rows, models aim at 55–65 words. Every situation, cue, table, criterion, sample, quote, model and image brief was written for this project; official material was used for shape only (timing, the "U hoort eerst …", "Kijk naar de plaatjes", "Gebruik alle drie de plaatjes", "Bekijk de tabel" and "Noem twee redenen / minstens twee punten" instruction patterns). No official scenario, picture or table was reused.

Level labels are authoring targets (`targetLevelValidated: false`). Nothing here claims official equivalence or a pass prediction. The rater scales (precondition, content, word choice, vocabulary, sentence formation, pronunciation, tempo) live in the `b1-spreken` rubric; the criteria carry only content checks (a piece of information, or a speech act with a count). The two instruction tasks keep the 017 machinestoring form of criterion 3 (ordered instructions to the addressee rather than a description of the pictures), which the official scale counts as content.

Conventions used in every task:

- Fields: `id`, `level: B1`, `part: speaking`, `exam: nt2-i`, `taskType: middellang`, `domain` (werk, educatie or overig), `title`, `status: draft`, `targetLevelValidated: false`, `rubric: b1-spreken`, `prepSeconds: 15`, `speakingSeconds: 30`, `prompt`, `cue` (two tasks: `{speaker, role, text}` with a role from `config/voices.json`), `images` (`{brief, alt, kind: drawing, size?}`) or `table` (`{caption, columns, rows}`, the 017 shape), `criteria` (`[[nl, en], …]`, four per task), `sample`, `quotes`, `model`. `size` is `sequence` for the two three-picture tasks, `pair` for the two-picture instruction and absent (default `single`) for the one picture.
- Prompts state the situation first and the instruction second in the exam's own patterns; no sentence over 21 words; 46–73 words in total (the two table tasks and the container task carry the situation facts the criteria refer to).
- Cues are 32 and 34 words of natural spoken Dutch that state the other person's position or question, numbers in words, no fillers; the models paraphrase and never read the cue back.
- Criteria name the speech act and the required count plus the content details the prompt asks for. None checks tense, connectors or sentence shape.
- Every sample is on topic, meets all criteria but one (that quote is `null`; quotes are verbatim substrings of the sample), opens with its own sentence rather than the prompt's, and contains exactly two typical learner errors of the kinds the brief lists (a fronted element without inversion, a missing article, a wrong perfect auxiliary, a verb not at the end of a subordinate clause). The errors are listed per task in section 2.
- Models are 58–65 words of B1 spoken Dutch: subordinate clauses (omdat, zodat, toen, als, want), connectors (daarna, ten eerste/ten tweede, bovendien, pas, gelukkig), modal advice forms (ik zou …, probeer …, u kunt …), consistent tense in the narrations, no sentence over 22 words.
- Cast from `config/illustration.json`, named in the briefs exactly as in the config with the three traits in brackets verbatim in every panel: Roos (3 panels), Karim (3 panels), Fatima (2 pictures). Non-cast characters (a baker, a lost-property employee, a volunteer, a man with round glasses and a green sweater who recurs in panels 2 and 3 of the taalcafé) carry two or three traits repeated verbatim where they recur. Cue speakers without a picture: a verhuurder (function label, `m-older`) and Mila (§8 name, `f-young`). Two more prompt characters are named without a picture: Sabrina (cast) as the colleague and Fatima as the new neighbour.
- No organisation, school, shop or company is named anywhere (the courses are "Cursus 1/2" at "dezelfde avondschool", the subscriptions "Abonnement 1/2", the office "het bureau voor gevonden voorwerpen", the café "het taalcafé" of "een taalschool"), so the §13 web check of invented names had nothing to check. No brand names, no real addresses, no text, logos, stickers, route numbers or signs in any picture.

## 5. Doubts for the reviewer

1. **Domain of cursus-engels and werktelefoon.** The coordinator asked for werk 3 / educatie 2 / overig 3 with subjects that split naturally 2 / 1 / 5 (interview advice and the fire alarm are werk; the taalcafé is educatie; wallet, landlord, containers and a phone subscription are overig). Two tasks were framed to reach the counts: *werktelefoon* is a work phone chosen at the manager's request against a company budget, with the calls and the navigation as work needs (werk); *cursus-engels* puts the candidate in the learner role at the same avondschool and makes the subject a course choice (educatie), although the addressee is a colleague. If the reviewer reads *cursus-engels* as werk because of the colleague, the counts become 4 / 1 / 3 and the smallest fix is to make the addressee "uw medecursist Sabrina" (prompt, criterion 3 unchanged); if *werktelefoon* is read as overig, the alternative is to move it to a fellow-student frame, which is weaker.
2. **taalcafe-avond and educatie.** The candidate is a learner at the taalschool, Karim a fellow learner and the setting the school's own taalcafé; that is the opleidingsdomein as the exam situates it (setting and role), which is why the setting was moved from the library (027) to the taalschool. If the 017 rule for eerste-schooldag (an observer narrating someone else's day) is applied strictly, the task becomes overig and the counts 3 / 1 / 4.
3. **Two narration tenses.** *portemonnee-tram* is told in the past ("Vertel wat er gebeurde"), *taalcafe-avond* in present narration ("Vertel wat er gebeurt"), the split the 017 review accepted so that drills carry both forms. Tense consistency is left to the `b1-spreken` feedback, not to a criterion.
4. **Prompt length.** *cursus-engels*, *afvalcontainers* and *werktelefoon* are 70–73 words (seven or eight short sentences) because the criteria refer to situation facts (money, children, certificate; Monday emptying and taking turns; the twenty-euro budget, calls and navigation). No sentence exceeds 21 words. If the coordinator wants prompts under 60 words, the facts can move to a `cue` (a colleague, a neighbour or a manager stating them), which changes the stimulus count.
5. **Sample lengths.** Samples are 46–58 words, a little above the 017 range (40–54) because each must show three met criteria with enough substance to be unambiguous. None is longer than its model.
6. **Cue lengths.** 34 and 32 words against 29 in 017. The landlord's cue needs the three weeks and the heater proposal (both are criterion material); Mila's needs the first-interview fact and the nerves. Both can lose one clause if the reviewer prefers shorter cues ("Dat is toch geen probleem?" and "mijn allereerste").
7. **afvalcontainers colour scheme and the shared pair.** Grey for residual waste, green for gft and blue for paper is the common Dutch scheme but not universal; the task names no municipality and the prompt only fixes the grey container. The pair shows the same person, the same container and the same building, but the step moves from behind the building to the kerb, which is inherent to "when to put it out"; a pair that stayed behind the building could not show the kerb.
8. **Sample and model disagree in cursus-engels.** The sample recommends course 2, the model course 1, each with two table points and a link to Sabrina's situation; either advice meets the criteria and the feedback must accept both. The same holds for either subscription in *werktelefoon*.
9. **brandalarm picture is a diagram.** Blueprint §7 says tables, forms, schedules and maps are real text in the app, never images; an evacuation plan without text is a picture of a pictogram poster, which the coordinator asked for. The brief keeps it to the house style (few shapes, palette colours, no letters). If the review wants a scene instead, the alternative is a colleague looking at the plan on a corridor wall, with the plan drawn as described.
10. **Names across concurrent batches.** Batches 026–030 (written in parallel) use Farah, Jari, Ricardo, Julio and mevrouw Bakker as cue characters and Sabrina in an A2 interview sequence. This batch uses Mila (§8 list, unused elsewhere), the function label "Verhuurder", an unnamed new colleague, and Sabrina as a colleague without a picture; Roos, Karim and Fatima appear in pictures with their config traits.

# Batch 029: design notes (B1 Schrijven, two deelschrijftaken and two korte schrijftaken)

Batch 029 contains four original B1 writing tasks in two of the three task types of Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I Schrijven, as described in `content/blueprint.md` §4.8: two deelschrijftaken (one form with three line fields and three open questions, one e-mail completion built on three pictures) and two korte schrijftaken (each an e-mail with a printed header, a table that is not part of the e-mail, a stated goal and five or six content bullets). Together with the eight zinstaken of batch 028 they make one 8 + 2 + 2 form. Every situation, form question, table, criterion, sample, quote and model was written for this project; official material was read for structure only (task shape, "Geef minimaal twee redenen", "Gebruik alle plaatjes", the sentence pair that the table is not part of the e-mail).

Level labels are authoring targets (`targetLevelValidated: false`). Nothing here claims official equivalence or a pass prediction. Feedback follows the official scales in order (adequaatheid 0–3, grammatica 0–2, spelling, samenhang and woordgebruik 0–1); the criteria carry the adequacy elements only.

Lessons from the batch 015 review applied here:

- Every invented organisation name was searched on the web before use, and its e-mail domain looked up in DNS and the SIDN registry (section 6). Nothing collides with a business.
- The picture task's criteria narrate the three pictures, one per picture and in picture order, and its prompt asks for every scored element: the three pictures ("Gebruik alle plaatjes") and the closing question. The app hides the criteria and the starters of a deelschrijftaak with images while the learner writes (`app/components/OpenExercise.tsx`, the picture-task condition), so the prompt and the pictures carry the whole task.
- Form labels are exactly as wide as the criteria that score them ("Wanneer kunt u niet werken? Geef een reden." ↔ "wanneer u niet kunt werken en geef daarvoor een reden"); the line fields ask for a name, a phone number and a date, never for an organisation name.
- The korte schrijftaken spend every bullet on content; no closing-formula criterion.
- Names shared with batch 028 (the zinstaken of the same form) were avoided: 028 has a child called Jari and a landlord called Kuipers, so the friend here is Ricardo and the coordinator Verhoeven.

Conventions used in every item:

- E-mail models are the body only: the header, salutation and closing are printed by the scaffold, so the learner writes what comes between "Hoi Ricardo," / "Beste meneer Verhoeven," / "Beste mevrouw Aydin," and the closing line. The name under the closing is not scored.
- The form model (uitzendwerk) gives the three open fields in field order as full sentences, without the labels, as in batches 008 and 015; the three line fields (name, phone number, date available) are invented by the learner and are not criteria.
- Criteria address the learner with "u", as the exam bullets do, also for the informal e-mail; the register of a task is set by the scaffold and the model.
- Every sample fulfils all criteria but one (that quote is `null`) and contains exactly two typical B1 learner errors, listed per task below. Quotes are exact substrings of the sample. Models are correct and complete and use subordinate clauses and signal words (omdat, terwijl, zodat, toen, ten eerste / ten tweede, daarom, bovendien, echter).
- No sentence of a prompt occurs in a sample or model (checked by sentence comparison in the author script); the official rule that copied sentences earn nothing is the reason.
- Cast from `config/illustration.json` and blueprint §8: Roos (the writer's persona in the three pictures, traits verbatim in every brief), Ricardo (the friend, not pictured), meneer Verhoeven (course coordinator), mevrouw Aydin (manager). Invented organisations: Uitzendbureau Kortemeer, Taalcentrum Westerbrug, Drukkerij Bladwijzer.
- Dates were checked against the 2026 calendar: vrijdag 13 november, vrijdag 11 december, vrijdag 25 september. Times are written as the exam writes them (19.00–20.15 uur, 06.00–14.30 uur); prices as € 95, € 429, € 524.

## 2. Per task: how the model meets each criterion, the omitted criterion in the sample, the sample errors

### 1 uitzendwerk (`B1:writing:batch029-uitzendwerk:1`)

Form: three line fields (Voor- en achternaam, Telefoonnummer, Beschikbaar vanaf (datum)) and three open fields; the criteria are the three open fields, in field order. The prompt gives the situation (a temporary contract ended last month, registration with the agency) and the official instruction form; the kind of work, the experience and the unavailable days are invented, as "Sommige gegevens moet u zelf bedenken" says.

- Model (135 words, nine sentences, average 15.0, longest 21; open fields only, in field order): (1) work in a restaurant kitchen or a care-home kitchen, with two reasons marked "Ten eerste … Ten tweede …": trained cook who wants to keep doing what he or she does best, and a fixed team to learn Dutch faster; (2) six years as a cook before coming to the Netherlands, the last two as chef-kok, then a year at a hotel making breakfast for about a hundred guests, contract ended in August; (3) "Op dinsdag- en donderdagochtend kan ik niet werken, omdat ik dan tot twaalf uur Nederlandse les heb." plus Sunday off for the children and availability on all other days and evenings.
- Sample (76 words) omits criterion 3: "Op dinsdag en donderdag kan ik niet werken. De andere dagen kan ik altijd." names the days but gives no reason, which the label and the criterion both ask for. Errors: "omdat ik ben kok van beroep" (finite verb not at the end of the omdat-clause) and "in keuken van een hotel" (missing article, which the official scale counts). The two reasons of criterion 1 are "ik ben kok van beroep" and "ik vind het leuk om voor veel mensen te koken", in two sentences so that the second is unambiguously a main clause.

### 2 kustweekend (`B1:writing:batch029-kustweekend:1`)

Pictures (three separate generations, `size: sequence`, Roos with her three fixed traits "young woman, dark hair in a bun, yellow scarf" in every brief, the same dyke and coast in all three): (1) on the path on top of a grassy dyke, Roos beside her bicycle looking at the visibly flat rear tyre, grey sky; (2) a simple bus shelter at the foot of the same dyke, Roos under the roof with the bicycle, heavy slanted rain and puddles, no timetable or signs; (3) a bench on the quay of a small harbour with fishing and sailing boats, Roos eating fries from a paper cone, bicycle against the bench, sun in a clear sky. Ricardo is not pictured. The prompt does not name Roos: the pictured person is the writer ("ik"), as Hasan is the unnamed demonstrator in 017-machinestoring. Alt texts describe the scenes in Dutch; a writing task has no key to hide, so the flat tyre, the rain and the sun are in the alts, as the pictures of 015 were described.

- Model (134 words, eleven sentences, average 12.2, longest 22, "je"): (1) "Na een uur kreeg ik midden op de dijk een lekke band, terwijl er in de buurt geen fietsenmaker was."; (2) "Onderweg begon het ook nog hard te regenen, zodat ik met mijn fiets in een bushokje moest wachten tot het droog was."; (3) "Toen ik eindelijk in de haven aankwam, brak de zon door. Ik heb op een bankje tussen de boten een grote patat gegeten …"; (4) "Hoe was jouw weekend eigenlijk? En heb je zin om de volgende keer mee te gaan?" The bike is pushed to the village and repaired there afterwards, so the story needs no bicycle on a bus (Dutch buses do not take ordinary bicycles).
- Sample (82 words) omits criterion 2: it narrates the flat tyre and the fries in the sun and asks two questions, but never mentions the rain or the bus shelter — the learner who skips one of three pictures, the typical loss in this task type. Errors: "patat geëten" (past participle of eten is gegeten) and "Ik was blij omdat de dag was toch nog gezellig" (finite verb not at the end of the omdat-clause). "dus ik moest een uur lopen" (dus without inversion) is accepted usage, not counted.

### 3 groepsresultaten (`B1:writing:batch029-groepsresultaten:1`)

Table (four rows, five columns: Onderdeel, Les, Aanwezigheid, Geslaagd, Gemiddeld cijfer; caption names group E2, twelve cursisten, the mock exam of vrijdag 13 november 2026 and the average attendance since September): luisteren Monday 19.00–20.15, 92%, 11 of 12, 7,6; lezen Monday 20.30–21.45, 90%, 10 of 12, 7,3; spreken Wednesday 19.00–20.15, 85%, 9 of 12, 6,8; schrijven Wednesday 20.30–21.45, 55%, 4 of 12, 5,1. The one inference the learner can make is that attendance collapses at the second Wednesday lesson and that this is the part where eight of twelve failed; the proposal follows from it but is invented ("Bedenk dit zelf"). The writer is the group's teacher (docent Engels at a taalcentrum), the coordinator asked for the report, and the prompt names the three things he asks for (results, attendance, proposal), so every criterion is asked for.

- Model (173 words, eleven sentences, average 15.7, longest 26, "u"): (1) "een kort verslag over groep E2, die op 13 november het proefexamen Engels heeft gemaakt"; (2) listening 11 of 12 with 7,6, reading 10 with 7,3, speaking nine passed; (3) "Schrijven is echter een groot probleem, want daarvoor zijn acht cursisten gezakt en het gemiddelde was maar een 5,1."; (4) over ninety percent on Monday, 55 percent at the Wednesday writing lesson, and what the writer notices: students go home after the speaking lesson because the last bus leaves early; (5) swap the Wednesday order (writing at seven, speaking after) and extra writing assignments at home for those who failed. Understandable without the table: group, exam, parts, numbers and percentages are in the text.
- Sample (111 words) omits criterion 4: it reports the results per part, names the failed part with the number, and proposes half an hour of extra writing per lesson plus weekly homework, but says nothing about attendance. Errors: "negen cursisten heeft een voldoende" (congruence, plural subject) and "omdat veel cursisten schrijven nog te weinig zinnen" (finite verb not at the end of the omdat-clause).

## 4. Doubts for the reviewer

1. **Register split 3 formal / 1 informal.** Fixed per task by the batch brief (a form, a report and a proposal are formal by nature; only the e-mail to Ricardo is informal). Batch 028's zinstaken carry the informal share of the twelve-task form.
2. **The writer as a teacher (groepsresultaten).** The persona "U werkt als docent Engels bij Taalcentrum Westerbrug" is the only one who plausibly holds results and attendance figures for a whole group. An NT2 candidate can be a teacher of another language, and the report to a coordinator is a realistic B1 act, but the reviewer may prefer a student-side persona; that would need a new premise (a group representative has no attendance table), so the item would have to be rejected rather than repaired.
3. **The pictured person is the writer and is not named.** Roos appears in all three briefs (traits verbatim) but not in the prompt: the pictures show "ik", as in the official picture e-mails, and as Hasan is unnamed in 017-machinestoring. A learner writes "ik"; nothing in sample or model names her. If the reviewer wants the prompt to name her, "U bent Roos" is not the exam's form; a neutral alternative is a second unnamed cyclist, which would cost trait consistency.
4. **Picture 2 and batch 026.** The bus-shelter-in-the-rain scene was prescribed by the brief; batch 026 (A2 speaking, written in parallel) has a one-picture description task "Wachten op de bus in de regen". Different level, part and act (describe vs narrate), and Roos is not that task's character, but two rain-at-a-bus-shelter pictures now exist in the bank; the media producer may want them to differ in composition (this one has the bicycle and the dyke).
5. **magnetron criterion 1 "per dienst".** The model gives all three shift sizes; the sample gives only the 21 of the middagdienst and I count it as met, because the problem is the largest shift. Feedback should accept one shift's number; the criterion could read "voor hoeveel collega's dat is" if the reviewer wants no doubt.
6. **magnetron sample criterion 2.** The sample's two reasons are waiting and cold meals (one reason in two sentences) and lunch boxes that do not fit (the second). If the reviewer reads the cold meal as a separate reason, the sample has three, which does not change the verdict.
7. **kustweekend criterion 2 wording.** "dat u met uw fiets in een bushokje hebt gewacht" accepts waiting for the bus and waiting for dry weather; the model chooses the second so that no bicycle needs to travel by bus. A learner who writes "geschuild" instead of "gewacht" meets it in my reading; the judge sees the criterion text, not the picture.
8. **groepsresultaten table width.** Five columns is the widest table in the part (015 has four). It renders as real text and scrolls inside its container on a phone; if the reviewer finds it heavy, the "Les" column can be dropped and the lesson times moved into the caption, at the cost of the attendance inference being less visible.
9. **Prices and dates.** € 95 for a 1000-watt microwave and € 429 for a 250-litre fridge are 2026 retail prices for basic models; "vrijdag 25 september" as a reply deadline assumes the e-mail is written in mid-September 2026, consistent with "vorige maand" (August) in the uitzendwerk prompt and model. The groepsresultaten task is set in November 2026 (mock exam 13 November, exam 11 December), which is when a September course reaches its mock exam; a learner practising in September reads a task set two months ahead, as the exam's own dates are arbitrary.
10. **uitzendwerk line field "Beschikbaar vanaf (datum)".** Chosen for "availability" so that it does not overlap with open field 6 (when you cannot work). "Aantal uren per week" is the usual alternative on agency forms; either is a line field and not scored.
11. **Sample error types.** All eight sample errors are grammar (word order after omdat/dat, congruence, a missing article) except "geëten" (a participle form, which the official scale counts under grammar as word formation or under spelling). The 015 review preferred one spelling error per picture task; here the picture sample has "geëten" and a word-order error, which covers two aspects of the scale.

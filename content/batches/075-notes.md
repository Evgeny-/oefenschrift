## Key evidence and distractors

### `batch069-lesboek` (Tom, teacher)

- **q1 C, Uw boek.** Evidence "Neem je boek mee." A pen and B schrift are the other familiar lesson things a learner may expect to bring; the message names neither.
- **q2 A, Oefening vier.** Evidence "Daarna maken we oefening vier." B twee and C zes are familiar unmentioned numbers; the one written number is the only one in the text.

### `batch071-eend-tas` (Eva, friend)

- **q1 B, Met de trein.** Evidence "Ik kom vandaag met de trein." A bus and C auto are the other common ways to travel; "station" supports the train.
- **q2 C, Om twee uur.** Evidence "Ik ben om twee uur op het station." A drie and B één are neighbouring hours; there is no second time in the text.

### `batch071-plant-ogen` (Yara, colleague) — funny

- **q1 A, Om elf uur.** Evidence "Kom je om elf uur koffie drinken?" B tien is the usual coffee time a reader might assume; C twaalf is the next hour (lunch). Only one time is written.
- **q2 B, In de keuken.** Evidence "Ik ben in de keuken." A tuin and C winkel are other everyday places; the picture shows only the plant, not a room.

### `batch071-kat-werkt` (Jan, colleague)

- **q1 A, Thuis.** Evidence "Ik werk vandaag thuis." B Op kantoor is the expected workplace of a colleague, so it tests reading "thuis"; C In de winkel is another workplace. The key is the shortest option.
- **q2 C, Om vier uur.** Evidence "Kun je mij om vier uur bellen?" A twee and B vijf are familiar hours; one time only.

### `batch071-taart-staart` (Ali, friend)

- **q1 B, Op zaterdag.** Evidence "Ik ben zaterdag jarig." A vrijdag and C zondag are the days on either side, the typical weekend confusion.
- **q2 A, Zijn broer.** Evidence "Mijn broer maakt soep." B zus tests broer/zus; C moeder is another family member who might cook. Ali himself makes the cake, so the question needs the right person, not just "family".

### `batch075-kip-tuin` (de buurvrouw) — funny

- **q1 C, Uw tomaten.** Evidence "Hij eet al uw tomaten!" A bloemen and B appels are other things in a garden a hen might peck; neither is mentioned and the picture shows only grass.
- **q2 B, Tot zes uur.** Evidence "Ik ben thuis tot zes uur." A vier and C acht are neighbouring even hours. "Tot" in the question matches "tot" in the text, which is intended at this level.

### `batch071-hoed-les` (Samir and Lisa, classmates)

- **q1 C, Lisa.** Evidence "Lisa uit onze klas is morgen jarig." A Samir is the writer (tests who-is-who); B De docent is the other class person a learner may expect a class card for.
- **q2 B, Een kaart.** Evidence "Ik koop een kaart voor haar." A boek and C bloem are common birthday gifts. The title "Feest in de klas" and the balloon picture name neither.

### `batch071-winkel-sok` (de winkel)

- **q1 A, Op vrijdag.** Evidence "U kunt hem vrijdag ophalen." B maandag and C zaterdag are common shopping days. The question uses "halen", the text "ophalen"; both are list words.
- **q2 C, Veertig euro.** Evidence "U betaalt veertig euro." A dertig and B vijftig are the neighbouring tens. The other number in the text, "zes uur", is a time and cannot be confused with a price.

### `batch069-tafel-dragen` (Kofi, brother)

### `batch069-kopjes` (Anna, colleague)

## Doubts for the reviewer

- `kip-tuin`: "Hij eet al uw tomaten!" — "al" can be read as "all" or "already"; both readings keep the key. "Er loopt een kip in uw tuin" uses existential "er"; check it reads easily.
- `winkel-sok`: the coat is an order the learner placed earlier; this is implied by "Uw nieuwe jas is nu in de winkel" and not stated. The price was set at forty euro to stay realistic.
- `kat-werkt` is now a cat-free message (21 words, near the lower limit). The slug no longer describes it; the plan allows that.
- Time-place questions dominate (16 of 30). Several are neighbouring-hour choices; check that the batch does not feel repetitive in a set.
- `tafel-dragen` mentions "zaterdag" without testing it; `nieuw-paspoort` mentions "tien uur" without testing it. Both are natural in the message and do not compete with a tested fact.
- Tom (`lesboek`, `koffer-les`) and Kofi (`tafel-dragen`, `hond-feest`) recur as the same person in different sets; check that is acceptable rather than four distinct people.

# Batch 030: design notes (B1 Spreken, eight korte taken)

Batch 030 contains eight original B1 speaking tasks of the type `kort` (Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I, Spreken I, Deel 1: twenty seconds of speaking time after a spoken cue, no preparation time) as described in `content/blueprint.md` §4.9 and the "Spreken I" paragraph of `docs/research/exam-blueprints-2026-09-10.md`. The batch covers the speech acts the coordinator asked for: ask two questions (×2), describe a picture to someone who cannot see it (×1), state a preference with a reason (×1), give two instructions (×2), react to news (×1), decline an invitation with a reason (×1). Every situation, cue, criterion, sample, quote, model and image brief was written for this project. Official material was used for structure only: the instruction pattern ("U hoort eerst uw collega." followed by the speech act and its count) follows the official form; no official scenario, cue or picture was reused. The subjects of batch 016 (exam date and costs, a loose window handle, a rota board, a test on paper or computer, coffee-machine cleaning, medication timing, a colleague's retirement, an open day) were avoided. The sibling batch 031 (middellange taken) was not visible at the time of writing; per the coordinator there is no overlap concern beyond the avoid list. The untracked drafts 026 and 027 (A2 speaking), 028 and 029 (B1 writing) were read for slug and subject overlap: none of their slugs recurs here and no subject coincides (028 *aantekeningen* is about class notes, not notes for a presentation).

No invented organisation, shop, street or product name is used anywhere in the batch (the garage, the shop, the firm, the course and the café are generic nouns; no brand names), so the web check the author brief requires for invented names had nothing to check. Level labels are authoring targets (`targetLevelValidated: false`). Nothing here claims official equivalence or a pass prediction. The rater scales (precondition, content, word choice, vocabulary, sentence formation, pronunciation, tempo) live in the `b1-spreken` rubric; the criteria carry only the content checks.

Conventions used in every task:

- Fields: `id`, `level: B1`, `part: speaking`, `exam: nt2-i`, `taskType: kort`, `domain` (werk, educatie, overig), `title`, `status: draft`, `targetLevelValidated: false`, `rubric: b1-spreken`, `speakingSeconds: 20` (no `prepSeconds`), `prompt`, `cue` (`{speaker, role, text}`, role from `config/voices.json`), `images` (at most one: `{brief, alt, kind: drawing, size}`), `criteria` (`[[nl, en], …]`), `sample`, `quotes`, `model`. The files are serialised with `JSON.stringify(…, null, 2)` plus a newline, as batch 016.
- **Three criteria per task, all asked for by the prompt.** Following the batch 016 review policy, criteria 1 and 2 realise the speech act and its count, and criterion 3 is one further element that the prompt names explicitly ("Zeg ook …", "Noem ook …", "Stel ook … voor") and that is either observable in the answer or in the picture: why you need the car soon, which topic you chose, what you think of the room, an advantage of the way you did not choose, where the food is (in the picture), whom to call if something fails, a proposal to celebrate, a proposal for another time. A learner who does what the prompt says meets every criterion; no criterion asks for an interpretation the picture cannot confirm.
- Prompts state the situation first, then "U hoort eerst …", then the speech act with its count ("Stel twee vragen: …", "Geef twee instructies: …", "Zeg wat u liever doet en waarom", "Reageer op het nieuws", "Sla de uitnodiging af en zeg waarom"), then the "ook" sentence for criterion 3. Tasks with a picture end with "Gebruik het plaatje." Prompts run 36–56 words.
- Cues are spoken Dutch of 20–27 words that open the floor without giving the answer; numbers in words ("acht uur"); no fillers. `u` from the garage employee and the docent (the 016 review kept `u` from teachers); `je` from the friend, the new colleague, the colleague and the friend from the course; the manager says `je` to the employee (as in 017); mevrouw Bakker's line uses only "jullie" and the learner answers her with `u`, as batch 009 has her speak.
- Models are 43–45 words in three to five sentences of B1 Dutch with subordinate clauses and connectors (omdat, want, als, waar, zoals, dus, zeker omdat), indirect questions ("wat de reparatie gaat kosten", "wanneer ik hem kan ophalen", "hoeveel minuten … moet duren", "hoe de wedstrijd was"), a conditional with inversion ("Gaat er iets mis, bel dan …"), a polite imperative ("Geeft u haar …"), a passive perfect ("dat is al weken geleden afgesproken") and a proposal with "zullen we". No prompt or cue sentence is repeated: the longest word sequence shared between prompt-plus-cue and any sample or model is three words ("in een team", "in één keer", "een andere keer", "wat de reparatie", "moet duren en").
- Every sample is on topic, fulfils all criteria but one (that quote is `null`) and carries exactly two typical learner errors (section 2). Quotes are exact substrings of the sample. Samples run 31–37 words, the length a learner at two words per second needs for twenty seconds.
- Pictures: two tasks have one picture each (the describe task and one instruct task). No cast member appears in a picture, so no cast traits are needed; both are object pictures without text, checked with the same `includes` test that `expandCast` in `scripts/illustrate.ts` uses. Cast names in cues (Julio, Mevrouw Bakker) and §8 names (Farah, Jari, Ricardo) only name the speaker.

## 2. Per task: speech act, cue role, the omitted criterion and its errors, how the model meets each criterion

1. **uitlaat** (`B1:speaking:batch030-uitlaat:1`). Speech act: ask two questions. Cue: the garage employee, `u`, role `m-shop` (Jerry: "repair service"), 27 words; he recognises the caller because the prompt says the learner gives their name, and says only that the car is still in the workshop, so neither the price nor the readiness is given away. Sample (33 words) omits criterion 1: it never asks what the repair costs. Errors: "Vrijdag ik moet" (verb second violated after a fronted adverbial) and "in andere stad" (missing article). 2. **eindpresentatie**. Speech act: ask two questions. Sample (31 words) omits criterion 2: nothing about notes. Errors: "interessant onderwerp" (missing article) and "Ik ben thuis al veel informatie gezocht" (wrong perfect auxiliary). Model (45 words, three sentences, average 15.0, longest 18): "Kunt u me vertellen hoeveel minuten de presentatie ongeveer moet duren?" ## 6. Doubts for the reviewer

1. **rijexamen labelled `educatie`.** The coordinator's counts (werk 4, educatie 2, overig 2) with these eight subjects leave the driving-test task as the second educatie item, so the situation is set in the learner's taalcursus (Farah is a fellow student, the news comes before the lesson) and the news is an exam after lessons. In the NT2 sense educatie is the candidate's own education; if the reviewer reads this as `overig`, the spread becomes 4/1/3, which the coordinator would have to accept.
2. **teamwerk and the A2 item *alleen-samen* (009).** Same underlying preference (alone or together). The differences: a spoken cue from a manager about one concrete project instead of two pictures, a B1 reason, and a concession as the third element ("één voordeel van de manier die u niet kiest") instead of "iets over het andere plaatje". If judged too close, the third element can become "Zeg ook wat u nodig hebt om goed te kunnen werken" with the prompt sentence adjusted; the sample's `null` at 3 would still hold.
3. **Two proposals as criterion 3** (rijexamen: propose to celebrate; voetbalavond: propose another time). Different acts, but the same move. Alternative for voetbalavond: prompt "… Sla de uitnodiging af en zeg waarom. Bedank hem ook voor de uitnodiging." and criterion 3 "Bedank Ricardo voor de uitnodiging." / "Thank Ricardo for the invitation."; the sample's "Leuk dat je het vraagt" would then satisfy it, its `null` at 2 (no reason) stands, and the model's "Wat leuk" would need "Bedankt voor de uitnodiging" in front (adding three words, 47, so drop "meteen" and "anders").
4. **Register across the cue and the model.** The manager says `je` and the model avoids an address form; mevrouw Bakker says only "jullie" and the model answers with `u` (as in 009); the docent says `u` (016 review decision). If the reviewer wants every cue and model in one register, the manager's cue can be rewritten with `u` ("kan ik u in een team zetten, of u werkt er alleen aan. Wat past het beste bij u, en waarom?", 27 words).
5. **The garage recognises the caller.** The cue says "u bent de klant met de kapotte uitlaat"; the prompt therefore states "U belt de garage en noemt uw naam." so that the recognition is motivated (batch 016's landlord cue works the same way). If the reviewer prefers a cue that presupposes nothing, "Goedemiddag, met de garage, u spreekt met de werkplaats. Zegt u het maar, wat kan ik voor u doen?" (18 words) fits, and the prompt sentence about the name can stay or go; the learner then identifies the car in passing, which no criterion scores.
6. **uitlaat and the A2 listening item *garage* (022).** Both are calls to a garage about a car. The A2 fragment is receptive, about brakes, a battery and tyres, and gives the pick-up time; this task is productive, about an exhaust, and the learner must ask the price and the readiness. Accepted as different acts and levels in the author's view.
7. **kantoortuin picture without people.** The office is empty because Julio calls in the lunch break, which the sample's "iedereen zit in de kantine" repeats; a person would add trait work without content. The `alt` necessarily describes the room, as the 016 roosterbord alt did.
8. **Small errors that could look like typos.** "Bij de raam" (kantoortuin) is a deliberate article-gender error; "Ik vind belangrijk dat" (teamwerk) and "Ik vind jammer" (voetbalavond) are the dropped "het" that the official scale counts as a larger error. If the reviewer prefers only the three error types of batch 016: "Bij de raam" can become "Bij raam" (missing article); "Ik vind belangrijk dat je elkaar kunt helpen" can become "Ik vind het belangrijk dat je elkaar kunt helpen" with "In mijn vorige baan ik heb ook altijd met collega's samengewerkt" (verb second) as the second error; "Ik vind jammer, ik hou erg van voetbal" can become "Ik vind het jammer, ik hou erg van voetbal" with "in de café bij het station" (article gender) as the second error.

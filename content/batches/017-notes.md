# Batch 017: design notes (B1 Spreken, eight middellange taken)

Batch 017 contains eight original middellange spreektaken (`taskType: middellang`) for Deel 2 of Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I Spreken, written against `content/blueprint.md` §4.9 (field shapes), §6 (voice roles), §7 (illustrations), §8–10 and the "Spreken I" paragraph of `docs/research/exam-blueprints-2026-09-10.md`: 15 seconds of preparation and 30 seconds of speaking, a spoken cue or none, one to three pictures or a table, and one of the Deel 2 speech acts (narrate three pictures, convince with two reasons, advise with at least two points, instruct step by step, compare two options and choose). A sibling batch (016) covers the korte taken. Every situation, cue, table, criterion, sample, quote, model and image brief was written for this project; official material was used for shape only (timing, the "U hoort eerst …" and "Gebruik alle plaatjes" instruction patterns, the counted requirement "Noem twee redenen"). No official scenario, picture or table was reused.

Level labels are authoring targets (`targetLevelValidated: false`). Nothing here claims official equivalence or a pass prediction. The rater scales (precondition, content, word choice, vocabulary, sentence formation, pronunciation, tempo) live in the `b1-spreken` rubric; the criteria carry the content checks (a piece of information or a speech act with a count). Since the editorial review no criterion checks tense or connectors (those belong to the `b1-spreken` feedback under sentence formation and clarity); the instruction task's criterion 3 asks for ordered instructions rather than a description of the pictures, which the official scale counts as content.

Conventions used in every task:

- Fields: `id`, `level: B1`, `part: speaking`, `exam: nt2-i`, `taskType: middellang`, `domain` (werk, educatie or overig), `title`, `status: draft`, `targetLevelValidated: false`, `rubric: b1-spreken`, `prepSeconds: 15`, `speakingSeconds: 30`, `prompt`, `cue` (two tasks: `{speaker, role, text}` with a role from `config/voices.json`), `images` (`{brief, alt, kind: drawing, size?}`) or `table` (`{caption, columns, rows}`, the shape of the batch 015 writing tables), `criteria` (`[[nl, en], …]`), `sample`, `quotes`, `model`. `size` is `sequence` for the two three-picture tasks (three separate generations per the `sequence` note in `config/illustration.json`, each brief repeating the character's traits verbatim and naming the same setting), `pair` for the two-picture instruction, and absent (default `single`) for the one picture.
- Prompts state the situation first and the instruction second, in the exam's own patterns ("U hoort eerst uw leidinggevende.", "Kijk naar de plaatjes.", "Gebruik alle drie de plaatjes.", "Bekijk de tabel.", "Noem twee redenen.", "Noem minstens twee punten."). No sentence over 30 words.
- Cues are 29 words of natural spoken Dutch that state the other person's position or question; the model paraphrases and never reads the cue back.
- Criteria (3–4 per task) name the speech act and the required count ("Noem twee redenen", "Noem minstens twee tips", "Gebruik alle drie de plaatjes" as one criterion per picture) plus the content details the prompt asks for.
- Every sample is on topic, meets all criteria but one (that quote is `null`; quotes are verbatim substrings of the sample) and contains two typical learner errors of the kinds the brief lists: a fronted adverbial without inversion, a missing article, or a wrong perfect auxiliary. The errors are listed per task below for the feedback evaluation.
- Models are 62–69 words of B1 spoken Dutch: subordinate clauses (omdat, zodat, terwijl, als, toen), connectors (daarom, ten eerste/ten tweede, daarna, uiteindelijk, bovendien), modal advice forms (ik zou …, je kunt het beste …, u kunt … het beste …), consistent tense in the narrations, no sentence over 21 words.
- Cast from `config/illustration.json`, named in the briefs exactly as in the config (`mevrouw Bakker` in lower case mid-sentence) with the three traits in brackets, so that `expandCast` also appends them: Modibo (3 panels), Amina (3 panels) with Fatima as her mother in panel 1, Hasan (2 pictures), mevrouw Bakker (1). Non-cast characters (an interviewer, a teacher, a technician in a speech bubble) carry two or three traits. Cue speakers without a picture: a leidinggevende (f-adult) and Julio (m-young).
- No brand names, no real addresses, no text, logos, route numbers or signs in any picture; the whiteboard, the stop button and the container are blank.

## 5. Doubts for the reviewer

1. **Domain counts.** The brief asks werk 3, educatie 3, overig 2. With the assigned subjects the natural split is werk 3, educatie 2, overig 3, so *sportabonnement* was framed as an education task: the school arranges the student discount, the table has an "Afstand tot school" row and a fellow student asks. If the reviewer reads the NT2 domain "opleiding" strictly (the candidate as a learner), *sportabonnement* becomes overig and the counts change to 3/2/3; the task itself needs no edit. The same strict reading would move *eerste-schooldag* (a child at school, narrated by an adult) to overig; it is labelled educatie because the coordinator listed it so and "school of the children" sits under education in the app's needed list.
2. **glasbak is a neighbourhood setting.** The brief's general rule says no neighbourhood settings; the coordinator assigned this subject explicitly. It is written as a rule-and-report task (gemeente, boete, melden), not a neighbourhood initiative, and the advice stays generic. An alternative with the same criteria is a full paper container at the learner's workplace.
3. **Two narration tenses.** *trein-gemist* is told in the past (the story of last week), *eerste-schooldag* in present narration ("Vertel wat er gebeurt"); both are common in Programma I and the tense criterion accepts either as long as it is consistent. If one form is preferred for drills, the prompt of *eerste-schooldag* becomes "Vertel wat er gebeurde" and the model shifts to the past without other changes.
4. **Five criteria for eerste-schooldag.** Three picture criteria plus feelings-with-reason plus connectors/tense. The model meets all five in 67 words; if five is judged too many for 30 seconds, merge criterion 5 into criterion 1 ("Vertel in de goede volgorde wat er op het eerste plaatje gebeurt …") and drop one starter.
5. **Language-shape criteria.** Criterion 4 of *trein-gemist*, criterion 5 of *eerste-schooldag* and criterion 3 of *machinestoring* check the shape of the answer (one tense, connectors, instruction form) rather than a content item. They mirror the rater rules the research note records (describing instead of instructing caps content at 1) and give the learner something checkable; if criteria must stay purely content, they can be dropped without touching the samples, because every sample meets them.
6. **cursusverzoek register.** The manager says `je`; the model answers with `u`. Both are realistic in Dutch shops; a learner who answers with `je` is not penalised by any criterion.
7. **machinestoring: the callee.** The second picture shows a phone call with a technician in a speech bubble; the learner may name the technische dienst, the monteur or the chef. If the reviewer wants the callee fixed, add "de technische dienst" to the prompt ("wie hij daarna moet bellen: de technische dienst").
8. **fietskeuze and sportabonnement tables.** The numbers are invented and plausible for 2026 (€ 379 / € 549 for new city bikes; € 24 / € 39 per month with student discount). Both tables are built so that either option can be argued with two points; the models pick one side, the notes state that the other side meets the criteria too.
9. **Cast names in cues.** *examentips* uses the cast name Julio for a cue without a picture, as batch 009 did; *cursusverzoek* uses the function "Leidinggevende" (`f-adult`) so that the manager is a woman without importing a cast member's age.
10. **Invented details in models.** "het nummer hangt bij de telefoon" (*machinestoring*) and "de glasbak bij de supermarkt" (*glasbak*) are not in the pictures. They are the kind of plausible addition a B1 speaker makes; learners are not required to produce them.
11. **Sibling batch 016.** Its subjects (study adviser, landlord, schedule board, a preference, coffee machine, medication timing, reacting to news, declining an invitation) do not overlap with this batch; neither do batches 012, 013 and 015 (bus driver interview, mantelzorg, evening course to team leader, hospital kitchen instruction, evening course form, missed workday, wardrobe complaint, break rota).

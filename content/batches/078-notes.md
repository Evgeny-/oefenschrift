## Key and distractor audit

### `gemeente-foto` — ordinary
- q1 A `Een foto.` — "Neem morgen een foto mee, graag." B `Een brief.` and C `Een formulier.` are other papers people often bring to a counter; neither is mentioned. The old passport is deliberately not mentioned, so only one thing to bring is stated.
- q2 B `Met de pinpas.` — "U betaalt hier met de pinpas." A `Met geld.` tests cash as the familiar alternative; C `Met de telefoon.` tests phone payment. Neither is said.

### `dokter-bellen` — ordinary
- q1 C `Koorts.` — "Hij heeft koorts." A `Pijn in zijn buik.` and B `Pijn in zijn oor.` are common child complaints at the doctor; neither is said. The key is the shortest option.
- q2 A `Om elf uur.` — "Komt u vandaag om elf uur met hem?", accepted by Nadia. B `Om twee uur.` and C `Om vier uur.` are other surgery times; only one time is said in the call.

### `bus-station` — ordinary
- q1 B `Bij het station.` — "Ik moet naar het station." A `Bij de markt.` and C `Bij het ziekenhuis.` are other common bus destinations; neither is said. The driver confirms with "dat kan" and does not repeat the place.
- q2 C `Drie euro.` — "Een kaartje kost drie euro, mevrouw." A `Twee euro.` and B `Vijf euro.` are nearby prices; only one price is said.

### `taxi` — ordinary
- q1 A `De Tulpstraat.` — "Mijn adres is Tulpstraat vijf." B `De Kerkstraat.` and C `De Molenstraat.` are familiar street names that are not said. The house number is not asked.
- q2 C `Om zeven uur.` — "Morgen om zeven uur, graag." A `Om acht uur.` and B `Om zes uur.` are the neighbouring hours; only one hour is said.

### `melk-zoeken` — ordinary
- q1 B `Naast de kaas.` — "De melk staat daar, naast de kaas." A `Naast het brood.` and C `Naast het water.` name other supermarket products that are not said.
- q2 A `Links.` — "De kassa is links, bij de deur." B `Rechts.` is the opposite direction; C `Boven.` tests an upstairs checkout. Neither is said. The two locations belong to two different objects asked in two separate turns.

### `markt-appels` — ordinary

- q2 B `Vier euro.` — "Dat kost vier euro." A `Twee euro.` tests confusing the price with the number of kilos; C `Zes euro.` is another plausible price. No calculation is needed: the price is said.

### `jas-passen` — ordinary

- q2 C `Groen.` — "Een groene jas, graag." A `Blauw.` and B `Zwart.` are common coat colours, not offered or said. The picture shows only beige coats.

### `eerste-dag` — ordinary

### `twee-schoenen` — lightly funny
- q1 A `Zwart en bruin.` — "Eén schoen is zwart, de andere is bruin." B `Zwart en wit.` keeps one right colour and changes the other; C `Bruin en blauw.` does the same the other way. The learner must hold both colours from one sentence.
- q2 C `Hij lacht.` — "Ik moet lachen!" A `Hij huilt.` is the opposite feeling; B `Hij gaat naar huis.` is a practical reaction he does not mention. The picture shows neutral faces.

## Doubts for the reviewer

- Four questions ask for a clock time (`dokter-bellen`, `taxi`, `eerste-dag`, `zwembad-open`) and three ask for a price (`bus-station`, `markt-appels`, `zwembad-open`), all as the plan asked. Each item has only one time and one price, but check that the batch does not feel repetitive; sets 2 and 4 each contain two of these.
- `zwembad-open` pairs a clock time with a price in one item; `taxi` pairs a street name with a time. Check the memory load when heard slowly.
- `dokter-bellen` q2 and `feest-zaterdag` q2 take their evidence from a question that the other speaker then accepts ("Ja, dat is goed." / "Ja, dat doe ik."). The fact itself is said once.
- `twee-schoenen` q1 needs both colours from one sentence; the options keep one colour right in each distractor. Check that this stays fair.
- `kind-ziek`: a teacher answering the school phone ("met de school") is a simplification. "Groep" was avoided to spare learners the Dutch school-year system.
- `bus-station`: many Dutch city buses no longer sell tickets on board; asking the driver the price is still a real, common exchange.
- `melk-zoeken` uses "De verkoper" for a female supermarket worker; "medewerker" is not on the list.
- `dokter-bellen` q1: the key `Koorts.` is the shortest option; the two distractors share "Pijn in zijn …". Check that this does not signal the key.
